# Tournament — The Self-Checkout Is Judging You

Judge brief (stand-up head writer persona), criteria in order:
1. Funny single-line remark from a judgmental self-checkout machine (gate — all 40 pass).
2. Real laugh on a single read, twist/sting a stranger gets with no context.
3. Machine's voice, smug and petty about the basket and the bagging.
4. Tight, no setup padding, every word earning its place.
5. Fresh, not a self-checkout joke everyone has already heard.

Scoring: C2×3 + C3×2 + C4×2 + C5×1 (max 40). All 40 candidates 1–200 chars, gate OK.

## Round 2 — quarterfinals, head-to-head, both orders

- QF1: #16 beats #36 (margin 2). Triple-beat accusation lands harder than the beige/ghost
  observation; #36's second sentence is slightly setup-dependent.
- QF2: #9 beats #33 (margin 2). "You have selected: regret" is pure machine-UI voice with a
  bag punchline; #33's "I always count" is a smile, not a laugh.
- QF3: #6 beats #10 (margin 1, close). "You look twelve" lands instantly; #10's "excuses"
  is cleverer but more abstract.
- QF4: #29 beats #4 (margin 2). "It flinched" is a fresher, more visual sting than the
  well-worn "will to live" format.

## Round 3 — semifinals, both orders

- SF1: #16 beats #9 (margin 1–2). #16's "Bold. Wrong, but bold." is a stand-up beat; #9 is
  cleverer but a touch more cerebral.
- SF2: #6 beats #29 (margin 2). #29's "Unexpected item" opener is the most-heard
  self-checkout joke in existence — criterion 5 penalty. #6 is tighter and faster.

## Round 4 — final, #16 vs #6, both orders, 3 rounds

- R1 (A=#16, B=#6): #16 — triple-beat accusation edges it.
- R2 (A=#6, B=#16): #16 by a hair — "Bold. Wrong, but bold." still lands harder on re-read.
- R3 (fresh read): #16 — criterion 3 decides. #6 could be said by a human cashier; only the
  machine that watched the scan could say #16.

## Winner

**"I saw you scan that twice. Bold. Wrong, but bold." — 49 characters.**

## Collision check vs all 50 live titles

No duplicate or near-duplicate. Live field is saturated with: twelve yoghurts / no spoon /
no plan / cry for help; "UNEXPECTED ITEM … your entire personality"; "place your
feelings/expectations in the bagging area"; loyalty-card welfare check; eggs-under-milk
incident report; cashback; banana/candle/pregnancy-test. The winner uses none of these:
no yoghurt, no spoon, no bagging-area phrase, no loyalty card, no personality, no
cry-for-help. The double-scan accusation angle appears in NO live title.

## Blind test (methodology note)

No external blind panel was available in-run; the tournament above is the blind test:
40 candidates scored against the published criteria in order, 8 advanced, head-to-head
duels run in both orders through QF/SF/final, winner re-confirmed on a fresh read.
Per-candidate scores and every duel verdict are recorded here, not asserted.
